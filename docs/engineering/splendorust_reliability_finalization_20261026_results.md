26 October 2026. **The separately authorized finalization audit passed and independent receipt review accepted recovered verification.** The audit took 120.719 seconds through its owning acceptance record, within the frozen 900-second allowance. ## What this result establishes

Recovered verification covers all **200 natural games and 11,020 live decisions**, with **zero observed player failures, timeouts or forfeits**. Both players kept the one-second complete-response limit. Optimized64's slowest accepted move was 0.8542834 seconds; unchanged32's was 0.5134891 seconds. One optimized64 move reached the descriptive 0.700-second warning margin and remained valid. The already-passed 104 startup decisions and 48 selected decision reproductions were reused and authenticated, not rerun.

This is sustained observed reliability for these exact players and execution conditions. It does not prove that future timeouts are impossible, isolate a universal causal cure, or supply a new playing-strength estimate. Separate recovered acceptance closes the outstanding evidence checks; it does not rewrite any earlier attempt as successfully completed within its own original allowance.

## The next step

Recommend preparing a bounded one-second external calibration using the isolated optimized64 candidate, with unchanged32 retained as a control. Choose and qualify an independent opponent or small diverse set before freezing fresh games. No new comparison or reference-pointer promotion has been launched by this audit.

The earlier completed 64-versus-32 strength comparison already established a 64.25% score (paired 95% interval 58.25%-70.25%), including both deadline-related losses. Some later qualification wording described that strength question as unresolved; the completed strength report is the authoritative result. Repeating that same question is not automatically the most useful next study.

## The broader picture

The project aims for a strong, dependable Splendor player under one second per move. Earlier comparisons showed that more search with the same pretrained network improved strength. Rare deadline failures then became the obstacle, so the programme investigated communication, inference and supervision costs and qualified separated supervisors. This accepted reliability evidence complements the existing strength gain and gives a firmer practical candidate for testing against independent opposition. Such calibration will tell us whether improvements generalize beyond the related 32-simulation control. Further search increases require their own timing qualification; a low average time does not eliminate tail risk. Larger-data collection and training remain deferred.
